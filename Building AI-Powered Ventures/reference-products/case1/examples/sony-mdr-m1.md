# Worked example: verify a product announcement date

This example supports slide 57. It is a source-review exercise, not a fresh search for Sony's latest headphones. Primary page checked on 18 November 2026.

## Delegate

Verify when Sony announced MDR-M1, identify the product category and record what the announcement does not establish for a protective-case decision.

## Finding

| Field | Verified result |
|---|---|
| Brand / model | Sony MDR-M1 |
| Category | Closed monitor headphones for music creators and sound engineers |
| Source market | Sony Europe press centre |
| Announcement | 5 September 2024 |
| Availability wording | The announcement states availability from September 2024; this is historical launch information, not a live stock check. |
| Case-design measurements | This announcement does not provide enclosure dimensions or a case-fit drawing. A separate model-specific measurement source is needed. |
| Latest-model status | Not assessed by this narrowly scoped source review. |

Source: [Sony's original MDR-M1 announcement](https://www.sony.eu/presscentre/sony-launches-closed-monitor-headphones-with-high-sound-isolation-for-studio-sound-quality-anywhere).

## Evaluate

The model name and announcement date appear together in the original release. A recent search-index date, review or document download cannot establish a newer hardware launch. Driver frequency response does not supply the outer dimensions needed for case design.

## Coach and reuse

Keep article, announcement, availability and document-revision dates separate. Recheck the original release before describing an established model as new. Save that procedure in the rules, retain this dated finding with the run, and refresh facts on the next monitoring task.

This example does not claim that a bad first report was observed, that a controlled before/after experiment was run, or that no newer Sony product exists.
